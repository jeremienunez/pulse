# Frame the interface

Turn purpose and inspected material into a direction the user can assess. Reuse the current request, decisions and available project context.

## Establish the useful brief

Resolve these facts before designing the surface:

- Audience and situation: who arrives, what they know, and what they came to do.
- Outcome and actions: the information or task that leads, and how success can be observed.
- Content: actual copy/data/assets, realistic ranges, and which facts are fictional or illustrative.
- Boundaries: approved scope, existing commitments, platform/accessibility constraints and material states.
- Visitor mode: `persuade`, `operate`, `read` or `experience`, chosen for this surface's outcome.

Read existing `DESIGN.md`/`PRODUCT.md` when present, plus representative components, tokens and assets. A missing document does not erase an identity visible in the project. Keep settled facts; questions address material omissions such as an unknown audience, rather than starting with a menu of styles.

An extension inherits its system and resolves the new hierarchy, interaction and states. A precise accepted brief needs only its remaining choices. A new or explicitly replaced identity needs two or three directions before user selection and implementation.

## Inspect references when a choice is open

Select a small relevant set from the product's peers, contemporary work or its audience's cultural context. The task determines depth; a local defect or an established extension does not trigger a research quota.

For a newly opened expressive direction, follow the shared [cultural method](cultural-digging.md): inspected works, observation-induced branches and rebounds, a counterpoint, and consequences for this brief. Its trace and root substance review precede a recommendation. The current research remains the single owner of these proofs.

For each useful reference, retain:

1. The source and inspected scope: page, image, viewport or actual interaction states.
2. A concrete observation: reading order, grouping, density, typography, feedback or navigation.
3. Why the mechanism may serve this audience and outcome.
4. Its boundary: static capture, inaccessible state, different content or another platform.
5. The transformation for this task: what relationship transfers and what must change.

Distinguish an observed mechanism from a hypothesis. A gallery listing is a lead; inspect the work it points to before using it as design evidence. When inspection is unavailable, keep that limitation and resolve the choice through explicit assumptions or a targeted question. Neither a portal nor a style label supplies an inspected reference.

## Make the choice concrete

An anti-perfect direction can use a motivated departure from regularity: optical asymmetry, uneven rhythm, a rough edge or a material process. Identify the cultural or functional reason, the relationship that remains consistent, and the reading/control boundary. Assess that departure in the composition. A style label alone does not choose these parameters.

Describe each open direction through the same content: first-view hierarchy, composition, reading/task path, meaningful interaction and narrow-screen adaptation. Explain its reference transformation, fit and risk. Different palettes alone do not give a structural choice. A familiar pattern is useful when its content and usage support it.

Use sketches or prototypes proportionate to the decision. Obtain the actual user choice before building an unresolved identity; a recommendation or preselected option is not that choice. Retain the selected direction and its rationale with audience, facts, constraints, states and acceptance checks in the existing Pulse spec/decisions. Planning can then consume a brief it need not invent.
