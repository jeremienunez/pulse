# Five architectural rhythm recipes

> Retained method from `rhythmic-architectural-motion/references/shot-recipes.md` within `pulse:design`. The current Pulse run owns its decisions and evidence. [Local adaptations](visual-direction-adaptation.md) distinguish direction from external standalone recipes.


These prompts are original motion treatments **inspired by** the Reel's visual
vocabulary. They do not recreate its shots or assert its actual timing.

## Shared look bible

- Location: anonymous concrete housing estate (or adapt to any original city).
- Shapes: repeated modular blocks, dark recesses, rounded slab balconies,
  solitary bent lamp poles.
- Color: steel grey and weathered green-grey surfaces, muted teal cyan glow,
  storm clouds without overexposed highlights.
- Contrast: restrained blacks, readable concrete texture, cyan emissive accents.
- Motion grammar: geometry appears or offsets on discrete music cues while the
  camera stays relatively fixed inside each shot.
- Sound: use original/licensed electronic beat, not a presumed source rip.
- Output: typically vertical 9:16 with caption-safe zone and no watermark.

## 1. Stack / accumulative architecture

**Framing:** static wide view; horizon low enough that growth remains visible.
**Geometry:** original building block, consistent width and facade period.
**Event order:** add copy high above established silhouette; pop in on downbeat,
then brief settle (e.g. 2–3 frames); previous copies freeze.
**Useful shot prompt:**

> Weathered concrete tower blocks stand beneath a heavy green-grey sky,
> nearly symmetrical wide frame, 9:16. Every designated pulse, a matching
> architectural segment snaps into place above the stacked silhouette,
> extending it upward. Existing blocks remain fixed and unaltered.
> Consistent apartment-window size, vertical edges and perspective,
> low-contrast cyan daylight, stable camera, original urban atmosphere.

**Avoid:** windows becoming random textures; buildings stretching like rubber;
copy count oscillating.

## 2. Architecture recursion / sky-tunnel

**Framing:** fixed low-angle view looking into a square or rectangular opening.
**Geometry:** ring of four facades around open sky; nested rings use a
single vanishing center and coherent inward scale.
**Event order:** create one smaller nested ring at each pulse or half-bar.
**Prompt:**

> A locked-off upward view from a deep urban courtyard. Four concrete facades
> form a dark geometric frame around the stormy sky. On successive music cues,
> progressively smaller copies of the architectural frame appear within the
> central opening, forming a precisely aligned infinite tunnel. Windows and
> facade edges converge smoothly toward one vanishing point. Muted teal-green
> grade, solid weathered material, untouched original outer frame.

**Avoid:** spiraling indiscriminately, sky closure, mismatched vanishing points,
architecture moving when a new ring appears. If a rotation is desired, specify
a deliberate angle increment as an **optional creative variation**.

## 3. Window-grid reveal

**Framing:** slightly tilted crop of a large concrete facade.
**Geometry:** image-plane grid projected onto the building plane.
**Event order:** reveal cells left to right, checkerboard, column scan,
or selected batches on beat; preserve revealed cells.
**Prompt:**

> Static oblique detail of an austere concrete apartment facade with a
> measured rectangular grid of recessed window bays. On each musical pulse,
> one additional row of identical dark window frames becomes visible,
> following the perspective of the wall. All visible windows stay in place.
> Cold natural overcast lighting, stained concrete texture, restrained
> color, crisp modular edges. Do not change camera angle or facade plane.

**Avoid:** off-grid windows; reflections inconsistent with fixed light direction;
structural morphing.

## 4. Receding cyan lamps

**Framing:** one isolated street lamp dominating the near foreground; empty
sky/negative space to its right, one oblique vanishing line.
**Geometry:** instances along world-space curb; depth scale must decrease.
**Event order:** extend array toward depth, one lamp per pulse.
**Prompt:**

> One curved cyan-lit lamp post against a dense teal-grey cloud bank,
> near-empty vertical frame. At each specified beat, another identical lamp
> appears farther along the same curb in a receding diagonal line.
> New lamps become proportionally smaller and closer together toward the
> common vanishing point; each has a natural pool of cyan illumination.
> Stable camera and consistent lens, plausible atmospheric haze.

**Avoid:** floating poles, inconsistent curvature, changing lamp color,
larger distant lamps, accidental double shadows.

## 5. Downward balcony cascade

**Framing:** orthogonal or gently oblique facade detail so grid is legible.
**Geometry:** one rounded balcony module used across rows/columns.
**Event order:** animate a downward row-step on each pulse; decide whether
all rows translate together or each new row reveals while earlier rows hold.
**Prompt:**

> Telephoto architectural study of a repeating grid of rounded, weathered
> concrete balconies. In sync with marked musical pulses, one vertical row
> steps downward by one balcony interval, then settles, creating a cascading
> mechanical wave across the facade. Keep balcony curvature, rail spacing,
> perspective and cast shadows consistent; camera locked, cold teal atmosphere.

**Avoid:** sagging balcony structures, changing module count unintentionally,
infinite texture warp instead of discrete offsets.

## Montage order and dynamic arc

1. Stack adds **density**.
2. Tunnel introduces **depth**.
3. Windows accent a **precise grid**.
4. Lamps stretch rhythm into **perspective**.
5. Balconies finish with **flowing directional repetition**.

Cut on a marked downbeat only when aligned with the actual track.
Each shot should retain at least one stable reference plane so viewers
understand what changed.

## Optional website/portfolio hero adaptation

The patterns can create a tasteful **short hero loop**: slow building grid,
a few cyan windows reveal, then hold enough negative space for heading/CTA.
Reduce flash intensity, prefer muted loops, and respect motion-reduction
preferences in the site. Do not autoplay audio on the page.
