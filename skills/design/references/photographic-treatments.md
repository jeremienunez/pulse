# Four photographic treatments: from graphic idea to directed photograph

> Retained method from `photographic-art-direction/references/photographic-treatments.md` within `pulse:design`. The current Pulse run owns its decisions and evidence. [Local adaptations](visual-direction-adaptation.md) distinguish direction from external standalone recipes.


These are new, original applications of the four principles visible in the Reel.
They are **not** the original creator's shot list, camera data or lighting
instructions, and they should not reproduce the exact scenes frame by frame.

## 01 — Golden-spiral flow: intimate awe

**Thesis:** A lone observer experiences an unexpectedly vast event.

- **Hero:** a small illuminated face, with a sky phenomenon in the distance.
- **Blocking:** camera just behind the observer's shoulder at eye level; foreground
  silhouette and a pathway or line of roof edges carry attention toward the face
  and the sky. Construct one curved eye-path but do **not** force physical objects
  to match a mathematically exact Fibonacci spiral.
- **Camera look:** environmental medium-wide, e.g. a 28–50 mm full-frame *look*,
  depending on how much context is needed. Real focal length depends on working
  distance and camera format.
- **Light:** sky event provides intermittent backlight; a nearby warm practical
  permits a readable edge of the subject, if the story needs that detail.
- **Color:** cobalt night, soft amber skin accents; retain darkness.
- **Try:** place the important reaction near the desired spiral termination.
- **Avoid:** obvious cartoon spiral embedded in the rendered image, unrelated
  fill lights, overblown skies and artificial plastic skin.
- **Acceptance:** first read is “observer reacting”, second read is “scale of event”.

## 02 — Radial: entrancement / revelation

**Thesis:** A person feels caught in an immense luminous mechanism.

- **Hero:** head/gesture of figure beneath an overhead geometric light structure.
- **Blocking:** lower viewpoint looking slightly up; arrange existing struts,
  fixtures, beams or architectural spokes toward an implied center behind the
  subject without having them visually pierce the face.
- **Camera look:** moderate wide, not gratuitous fisheye distortion; preserve
  enough background to understand radiating architecture.
- **Light:** practical radial fixture is the motivation; use a very low soft
  lift on the face only if required, with matched direction/color.
- **Color:** neutral skin/garments, restrained colored practicals, no arbitrary
  second neon palette.
- **Try:** move subject slightly off the exact dead center if face readability
  improves while keeping the fixture's converging structure.
- **Avoid:** facial halo merging into the head, optical false symmetry, harsh
  blown LEDs or contradictory shadow directions.
- **Acceptance:** visible source explains the rim light; lines direct attention.

## 03 — Symmetry: ritual / institutional scale

**Thesis:** A tiny figure faces an imposing, impersonal space.

- **Hero:** solitary person on the center axis of a station or long arcade.
- **Blocking:** camera centered at pedestrian height; align left/right
  architectural lines toward one vanishing point. Keep just enough human
  imbalance (a step, shoulder tilt or slightly different light fixture) to
  avoid sterile mechanical perfection.
- **Camera look:** wide to normal depending on corridor depth and available
  camera position. Avoid invented absolute symmetry in an existing place.
- **Light:** long row of existing luminaires plus soft atmospheric haze if
  permissible. Preserve details in highlights and foreground shadow.
- **Color:** muted sodium-warm fixtures versus cooler ambient street sky.
- **Try:** move the **camera** rather than digitally warping the building when
  the axis is misplaced.
- **Avoid:** mirror-cloned pedestrians, duplicated signage and implausibly
  identical building wear.
- **Acceptance:** central vanishing point and figure share a legible hierarchy.

## 04 — Frame within a frame: separation / threshold

**Thesis:** Someone is physically near yet emotionally inaccessible.

- **Hero:** a lone human silhouette at the far end of a dark interior passage.
- **Blocking:** camera inside a doorway or underpass, foreground edges occupy
  a stable portion of the composition. Background aperture frames the figure
  without cutting off the essential gesture/prop.
- **Camera look:** locked medium-wide to preserve the foreground opening and
  depth; exposure prioritizes silhouette against the illuminated exterior.
- **Light:** existing exterior daylight is the motivated backlight; optional
  bounce in the passage only if a faint foreground texture is narratively needed.
- **Color:** deep neutral shadows, cool exterior greenery and luminous haze.
- **Try:** test whether the subject remains legible as a small image before
  deepening the shadows.
- **Avoid:** black foreground swallowing the scene or fake three-point lighting
  on a deliberately anonymous silhouette.
- **Acceptance:** viewer reads threshold → subject → world beyond.

## How to select the pattern

| Desired emotional beat | Candidate grammar | Key risk |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity / discovery | Golden flow | Rule forced onto scene |
| Energy / awe / hypnosis | Radial | Busy patterns swallow subject |
| Authority / ritual / alienation | Symmetry | Flat, inert rigidity |
| Intimacy / distance / discovery | Frame within frame | Foreground obscures information |

Rules can be broken intentionally. State *what the deviation adds to the image*.
