# Motion contribution

Describe the temporal need of the accepted surface. An interface communicates a state change or supports an action; a film composes attention over a timeline. Record the chosen domain and what the viewer should understand at each moment.

## UI interaction

For each animated change, specify its trigger, initial/final state, information, duration/curve, interruption and exit. Test the repeated or interrupted action, keyboard/focus continuity and reduced-motion presentation. Give immediate feedback when an action needs acknowledgement; the larger transition can follow.

Choose properties and timing from the actual distance, content and frequency. Existing application motion and the platform are useful context. Inspect the implemented movement and its static information state at the supported sizes; source values alone do not establish the experience.

For web craft, read the retained [motion prescriptions and recipes](awwwards-motion.md) and [implementation patterns](awwwards-patterns.md) before selecting treatments. Keep one concept-derived verb, concrete timing, one treatment per role, continuous state handoffs and the primary/utility distinction. Read the full touch, focus, idle, interruption and reduced-motion clauses; a short statement that motion should be useful does not supply these decisions. [Engine](awwwards-engine.md), [WebGL](awwwards-webgl.md) and chosen stack adapters remain optional technical recipes with adjacent [discordance notes](awwwards-adaptation.md#a11-local-recipe-discordances).

Interpret `colorOnly` and `noHoverChange` against the actual rest/hover/focus/active pairs, role and reduced-motion feedback. A raw flag is a signal to inspect, not an automatic failure or a requirement that the flag list be empty; a useful visible state may be deliberately static.

## Film and temporal composition

Start with a short beat sheet: what leads, what changes, and what the viewer retains. Define each shot's purpose, visual anchor, framing, movement and transition into the next. Allocate reading time from the actual text and image complexity. Inspect a rough timing pass before adding layers.

Use hierarchy over time: establish an anchor, introduce the next relationship, then release attention. Adjust cuts, holds, overlaps and contrast to the message and available footage. A motion decision should explain what changes in the viewer's understanding; activity alone supplies no narrative purpose.

Keep the beat sheet, measured timing and observations with the current brief. A guide can specify this composition without generating media. Asset selection and execution use their own accepted capability contracts and the current task authorization.

For architectural repetition, read [the complete rhythmic method](rhythmic-architectural-motion.md) and its [five recipes](rhythmic-shot-recipes.md): stable anchor, module, projection-constrained transform, event, ease and exit. Keep duplication distinct from camera zoom. In the [manual cue workflow](../../visual-assets/references/beatmap-workflow.md), distinguish nominal BPM/downbeat, measured onsets, ideal times and frame quantization/collisions. Shot labels, beat intervals, seconds and frame totals must agree before handoff; a provisional grid does not establish audio sync.

For narrated collage, read [the complete code-explainer method](code-animated-explainer.md), [asset preparation](explainer-asset-direction.md) and [production handoff](../../visual-assets/references/explainer-production.md). Let narration lead one visual idea per beat and 1–3 new events per phrase. Each independently moving object needs its own real alpha layer or mask. Keep camera transforms separate from objects, and pin captions/titles to screen coordinates outside those camera transforms. Example gestures include scale 0.85→1.0, words eased over 0.15–0.3 s and larger cutouts over 0.35–0.9 s, adjusted to reading and voice cues; these are film examples, never default UI timings.

The [reference project format](../../visual-assets/references/explainer-project-format.md) describes the supplied external renderer and its inspected limits. It supplies no automatic speech alignment, segmentation or lip-sync. Prepare actual cues and inspect an admitted preview before calling a video exported. A prompt-only request still receives its prompt, not an imposed production workflow.
