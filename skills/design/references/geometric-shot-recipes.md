# Six framing recipes

> Retained method from `geometric-composition-director/references/shot-recipes.md` within `pulse:design`. The current Pulse run owns its decisions and evidence. [Local adaptations](visual-direction-adaptation.md) distinguish direction from external standalone recipes.


These are newly written examples of observable compositions, inspired by the
six labelled demonstrations in the source Reel; they do not reproduce shots,
assets, watermarks, or software from the source.

## 1. Golden section with curved flow — revelation

**Story:** a person emerges through an attic hatch and looks at the world.
**Primary:** golden-section focal area, with real-world curving gesture/light.
**Camera:** medium low-height shot looking obliquely toward the open hatch.
**Place:** face toward the right/top golden-section region, e.g. (.618,.382),
with curved garment fold/hinge and light transition leading to it.
**Light:** soft outdoor glow, interior beams mostly in shadow.
**Avoid:** drawing a visible spiral in the final scene; awkward gaze/crop.
**Validation:** face becomes first focal point despite bright hatch and timber.

## 2. Rule of thirds — narrative asymmetry

**Story:** someone sorting boxes in a dark studio discovers one object.
**Primary:** hand and object near right lower intersection, useful negative
space to left for context or text.
**Camera:** medium view from shelf height, open depth toward the opposite wall.
**Avoid:** elbow clipped at frame or title colliding with the arm.
**Validation:** focal hierarchy should be readable without grid.

## 3. Circle — isolation

**Story:** a crouched figure surrounded by a soft circular area of cold light.
**Primary:** pool of light and partial arch make perceptual circular frame.
**Camera:** static wider shot from knee height, subject near center of aperture.
**Avoid:** artificial white ring in final image, blown highlights.
**Validation:** outside environment is legible enough to contextualize circle.

## 4. Center — anticipation

**Story:** a woman opens a doorway to look directly forward.
**Primary:** vertical doorway axis, face close to it, deliberate shadows left/right.
**Camera:** eye level; fixed enough to make the axis meaningful.
**Avoid:** labeling this “perfect symmetry” unless walls and light truly mirror.
**Validation:** gaze and doorway converge; no distracting objects at corners.

## 5. Scale — awe

**Story:** one human confronts a vast mansion and staircase.
**Primary:** readable person (small but not invisible) against a monumental mass.
**Camera:** wide establishing shot, stable vertical edges, subtle atmospheric depth.
**Avoid:** size claims measured from raw pixel ratios without perspective context.
**Validation:** is the person clearly seen at typical phone viewing size?

## 6. Triangle — tension through relationships

**Story:** a woman sees herself in a gilded mirror, hand on a table.
**Primary:** triangle linking eyes, mirror accent, hand; eye is brightest node.
**Camera:** gently oblique medium shot, leave negative space outside triangle.
**Avoid:** accidental three-way balance obscuring the main person.
**Validation:** visual hierarchy holds without drawn guides.

## When to use geometry vs story

Start with the user's **emotion and focal story** and then choose the
graphical principle. Never insist on strict mathematics at the expense
of human readability, eye-line, natural gesture or brand text-safe areas.
